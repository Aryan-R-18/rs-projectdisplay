About the project

Creating a 3D model of a physical object usually needs an expensive scanner. This project makes 3D modelling simple, affordable and efficient: basic mechanical parts and a standard camera capture photos of an object from many angles, and photogrammetry software turns them into a digital 3D model.

The object sits on a rotating platform, called the subject plate, driven by a stepper motor. A camera on a vertical mount moves up and down on a lead screw, driven by a second stepper motor. As the plate turns, the camera captures images at regular intervals and at different heights, and the software combines shared features across the images into a 3D structure. An Arduino Nano coordinates the whole process.

The tool is built from 3D-printed parts, standard electronics and open-source software, so it is easy to replicate. It suits designers, students, hobbyists, heritage archivists and game artists scanning small to medium objects indoors. Next steps include horizontal camera movement, CNC-machined parts, infrared lighting, wireless image transfer with a Raspberry Pi, and a 360° camera.
